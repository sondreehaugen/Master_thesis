This master's thesis marks the completion of our Master of Science in Industrial Economics at the Norwegian University of Life Sciences (NMBU). We would like to express our sincere gratitude to the individuals and organizations that made this research possible.

First and foremost, we would like to thank our main supervisor, Associate Professor Øyvind Lervik Nilsen, for his invaluable guidance and support throughout this period. His dual affiliation with the Norwegian Public Roads Administration (SVV) and NMBU provided a unique bridge between academic theory and practical application, and his close supervision was essential to the completion of this work.

We extend our thanks to our co-supervisor, Professor Kristian Hovde Liland at NMBU, for his technical expertise and assistance with complex machine learning challenges. We also wish to thank our co-supervisor, Hans-Petter Hoås Bauhr at SVV, for his insights into the strategic needs of the road administration, which helped shape our research into a project of high relevance for the sector.

Finally, we would like to express our gratitude to SVV for providing us with this compelling task and for their generous financial support during the project. This collaboration has provided us with deep insights into the future of digitalized transport systems.

On a personal note, we would like to thank our friends, family, and partners for their motivation and support throughout our entire journey at NMBU.

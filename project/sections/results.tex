\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter presents the empirical findings of the study, organized around the three research questions in \autoref{sec:rq}. Section~\ref{sec:training-results} evaluates the performance of fine-tuned YOLO26 detection models on a custom traffic dataset containing 1282 annotated frames collected at two intersections in Norway. Section~\ref{sec:tracking_results} presents tracking pipeline performance. It examines how different model-tracker combinations reconstruct vehicle trajectories and estimate O-D movements under varying lighting conditions. The evaluation includes benchmark comparisons on standardized metrics and validation against manually annotated ground-truth data.

\section{YOLO Results}
\label{sec:training-results}
This section presents the performance of the YOLO26n and YOLO26m models, fine-tuned for detailed vehicle classification at the intersections. Both models were trained on the custom traffic dataset and evaluated on an independent test set to assess their performance. 

YOLO26n was trained for 87 epochs before early stopping was triggered at a validation loss plateau. YOLO26m, on the other hand, completed all 100 epochs. Results are reported using standard detection metrics (mAP$^{50-95}$, mAP$^{50}$, precision, recall) to address the first research question on detailed vehicle classification.

\subsection{Model Training and Validation}
\label{sec:yolo_train_val}

The training progress of the YOLO26n and YOLO26m models was evaluated using mAP$^{50-95}$ and mAP$^{50}$. As shown in \autoref{fig:train_val_map}, both models exhibit a consistent upward trajectory. The YOLO26m model achieves a higher peak mAP$^{50}$ of 0.903, compared to 0.742 for the YOLO26n variant. Similarly, for the stricter mAP$^{50-95}$ metric, YOLO26m reaches a peak of 0.724, while YOLO26n peaks at 0.599. Performance for both models effectively plateaus after epoch 55, indicating they have reached their learning capacity for the dataset.

\section{Tracking Results}
\label{sec:tracking_results}
This section evaluates the MOT pipeline that integrates YOLO26 detections into vehicle trajectories and O-D movements. Four distinct experiments are presented: a 15-minute daytime segment for tracker configuration comparison; a 1-hour daytime rush-hour segment for best-configuration performance validation; a 1-hour nighttime segment under low-light conditions; and lastly, a 10-minute twilight segment validating adaptive switching between day and night tracker configurations. The evaluation measures class-level errors, route-level errors, and total vehicle count errors against manually annotated ground truth. This directly addresses the second research question on performance under varying lighting conditions. Additionally, the fourth experiment addresses the third research question by evaluating the system’s ability to operate autonomously during day–night transitions using adaptive tracker switching.

\subsection{Daytime Tracking Performance}
\label{subsec:daytime_tracking}

The daytime tracking comparison and evaluation used a 15-minute and 1-hour video recorded under daylight conditions at the Fv587 Haukeland intersection. Six model-tracker combinations were tested: YOLO26n and YOLO26m paired with three tracking algorithms (ByteTrack, default BoT-SORT, and a custom Day BoT-SORT). For each combination, the predicted O-D matrix was compared against the manually annotated ground truth.

\subsubsection{Comparative Results: 15-Minute Evaluation}

\autoref{tab:od_daytime_all} summarizes the results for all six model–tracker combinations. Overall, YOLO26m consistently outperforms YOLO26n across all tracker variants. For YOLO26m, performance is stable across trackers, with class error ranging from 0.8\% to 2.5\% and route error from 0.4\% to 1.0\%. In contrast, YOLO26n exhibits substantially higher errors, particularly for route estimation, where errors increase to 18.6\%.

\subsection{Nighttime Tracking Performance}
\label{subsec:nighttime_tracking}

To evaluate robustness under low-light conditions, the pipeline was tested on a 1-hour nighttime video segment. In addition to the standard tracker configurations, a custom
Night BoT-SORT Conservative variant was developed specifically for low-light conditions. Pedestrian detection is excluded from this evaluation because no pedestrian crossings occurred in the nighttime segment.

\subsubsection{Comparative Results: 1-Hour Evaluation}

Table~\ref{tab:od_nighttime_all} presents the performance of all six model-tracker
combinations evaluated on the nighttime segment. YOLO26m + BoT-SORT Conservative achieves the lowest route error (3.7\%) and total count error (1.5\%), predicting 132 vehicles against a ground truth of 134. YOLO26m + ByteTrack ranks second by route error (4.1\%), with a total count error of 3.7\%, followed by YOLO26m + BoT-SORT at 5.0\% route error and 4.5\% total count error. All three YOLO26n configurations produce substantially higher route errors, ranging from 22.9\% to 33.3\%, despite comparable or lower class errors. YOLO26n + BoT-SORT Conservative achieves the lowest class error within the YOLO26n family (8.2\%) and the smallest total count error in that group (2.2\%), but its route error of 22.9\% is the third highest.

\subsection{CPU Runtime Performance}
In the 15-minute daytime video segment, we benchmarked the most lightweight end-to-end configuration (YOLO26n + ByteTrack) to estimate the computational feasibility of operational deployment. The full pipeline (detection + tracking + O-D counting) achieved 67.6 FPS on a \mbox{NVIDIA Tesla T4} GPU (Google Colab) and approximately 14 FPS on CPU using the exported ONNX model with ONNX Runtime (Google Colab; 2 vCPUs on an Intel(R) Xeon(R) CPU @ 2.00\,GHz).
